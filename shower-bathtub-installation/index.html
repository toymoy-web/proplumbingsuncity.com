What We Install

We install new showers, bathtubs, and shower-to-tub or tub-to-shower conversions, including walk-in shower conversions, which come up often in a 55-plus community where accessibility matters more than in a typical household.

This includes the plumbing rough-in work, valve and drain placement, and connecting the new fixture, coordinated with your surround or tile installer if that's a separate part of the project.

Why This Comes Up Often In Sun City

A genuinely common request in Sun City is converting an original tub to a curbless or low-threshold walk-in shower, which is a real safety and accessibility improvement for a community built around older residents.

Original bathroom plumbing here also sometimes needs valve or drain relocation to fit a modern fixture properly, which is worth planning for before the project starts rather than discovering mid-installation.

Planning Ahead For A Bathroom Project

If you're planning a broader bathroom remodel rather than a single fixture swap, it's worth having us assess the plumbing early in the process, before flooring or tile decisions are finalized, since valve and drain placement can affect the layout options available.

For a walk-in conversion specifically, we'll also check the condition of the surrounding supply lines while we're in there, since it's a natural point to address anything original to the home that's showing early wear before it becomes its own separate call later.

If accessibility is the main goal, a curbless shower pan with a linear drain is worth discussing, since it eliminates the threshold entirely rather than just lowering it, which is a meaningfully different outcome for someone using a walker or wheelchair.

What To Do Before Installation Day

Clear The Bathroom

Remove personal items and anything stored under the sink or near the tub so we have full access.

Confirm Your Fixture Choice

Have the shower, tub, or conversion kit selected ahead of time to avoid delays mid-project.

Plan An Alternate Bathroom

If this is your only bathroom, arrange access to another one during the work window.

Grab Bar And Fixture Placement

While we're doing the plumbing rough-in, it's an efficient time to also plan for grab bar blocking behind the wall, since adding proper support after the tile is finished is a much bigger job than doing it during the initial installation.

The same applies to a handheld showerhead or a bench seat, both common additions in a walk-in conversion, since their plumbing and structural needs are far easier to plan for upfront than to retrofit later.

A thermostatic mixing valve is also worth considering during any shower installation in a home with older residents, since it holds water temperature steady and prevents the sudden scalding or cold-water surprise that can happen when someone else in the house runs water elsewhere.

It's a modest add to the overall project cost that makes a meaningful, everyday safety difference. Every rough-in we perform, valve height, drain slope, and fixture spacing, follows current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements.

We understand a bathroom being out of commission is a real inconvenience, so we sequence the work to get water restored to at least one fixture as early in the project as the layout allows.
