Hard Water Is What Peoria Homeowners Call Us About Most

Peoria's water is drawn from a mix of Central Arizona Project canal water and local groundwater wells, and it's independently reported as being among the hardest municipal water in the Phoenix metro. That shows up fast as scale on fixtures, reduced water heater life, and soap that won't lather.

Because that hardness is sitewide rather than tied to any one neighborhood's age, filtration and softener installation is our single most common Peoria call, more than any specific repair category.

The Old vs. New Peoria Divide

Peoria near the Sun City and Youngtown border, including neighborhoods like Fletcher Heights, developed mainly through the 1980s and 90s, putting it in roughly the same copper-pipe era as Sun City West.

Further north, Peoria's Vistancia area is 2000s-and-later master-planned construction on comparatively younger plumbing. We serve both, but the older section near Sun City is where copper-era leak and water heater calls concentrate.

Signs You're Dealing With Peoria's Hard Water

Chalky white buildup on faucets and shower glass that comes back within days of cleaning is the clearest visible sign, along with dishes that come out of the dishwasher with a cloudy film no rinse cycle removes.

A water heater that's scaling up and losing efficiency faster than its rated lifespan would suggest is another common Peoria complaint, directly tied to the mineral load passing through it daily.

Reduced water pressure that develops gradually rather than suddenly is also worth mentioning when you call, since it often points to scale narrowing pipes over time rather than a single blockage.
