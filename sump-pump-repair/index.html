Why Sump Pumps Are Rare Here, And Where They Show Up

Most Sun City homes are built on a slab with no basement, so the sump pump that is standard in cold-climate homes is the exception here. Where we do find one, it is usually tied to a specific low spot rather than a whole-house system.

The common cases are a drainage pit at the low point of a lot, a below-grade utility or storage area, a lower-level bathroom that needs a sewage ejector pump to lift waste up to the main line, and yard drainage or pool-equipment pits that collect runoff. Each works the same way, with a pump sitting in a pit and moving water out when a float switch lifts.

That is why a pump failure here tends to be discovered at the worst moment. A pit that sits dry for months has no reason to be checked until monsoon rain or a plumbing leak finally fills it.

Signs Your Pump Needs Attention

A pump that runs constantly without lowering the water level is usually undersized, clogged at the intake, or has a failed check valve letting water drain back into the pit.

A pump that never turns on while the pit fills points to a stuck float switch, a dead motor, or a tripped breaker.

Grinding, rattling, or a loud hum without any pumping are signs of a worn impeller or a motor about to fail, and replacing a pump at that stage costs far less than dealing with a flooded space afterward.

What To Do If Your Pump Fails During A Storm

Stay Out Of Standing Water Near Electrical

If water is pooling near the pump or outlets, do not step in it until the power to that area is off.

Shut Off Power To The Pump

Unplug it only if the cord and outlet are completely dry. Otherwise switch off the breaker.

Move Belongings Away From The Pit

Get boxes, stored items, and electronics off the floor near the low spot.

Sump Pump Or Sewage Ejector Pump?

The two look similar but do different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater or runoff out of a pit and away from the house. A sewage ejector pump lifts waste water from a bathroom or other fixture that sits below the main sewer line, and it works inside a sealed, vented basin.

We service both. Pump, basin, and discharge piping work follows Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for sumps, ejectors, and discharge lines, and we route discharge water away from the foundation so it does not simply return to the pit.
