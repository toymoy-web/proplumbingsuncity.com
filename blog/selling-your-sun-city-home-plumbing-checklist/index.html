Selling Your Sun City Home? A Plumbing Checklist

By the Sun City Pro Plumbing team · October 19, 2026

A little plumbing preparation before listing can prevent a buyer's inspection from becoming a negotiating headache, and it's worth knowing which issues to fix versus which to simply disclose honestly.

Why This Matters More For An Older Sun City Home

Buyers looking at homes in Sun City's original construction era increasingly know to ask about pipe material and plumbing age, which means an unaddressed issue that surfaces during a buyer's own inspection is more likely to become a real negotiating point than it would on a newer home elsewhere.

Getting ahead of this before listing, rather than reacting to a buyer's inspection report, generally gives you more control over both the cost and the framing of any plumbing conversation during the sale.

A Pre-Listing Checklist Worth Running Through

Test Every Fixture

Run every faucet, toilet, and shower to confirm normal function, not just that water comes out.

Check For Visible Leaks

Under every sink, behind every toilet, and around the water heater base.

Note Your Water Heater's Age

A unit near or past its typical lifespan is worth mentioning proactively rather than letting a buyer's inspector find it first.

Consider A Sewer Camera Inspection

Especially valuable on an original-era home, since it's exactly what a thorough buyer will likely request anyway.

What's Worth Fixing Versus Disclosing

A running toilet, a dripping faucet, or a slow drain are all inexpensive, quick fixes worth handling before listing, since they're the kind of small issue that makes a buyer wonder what else hasn't been maintained, out of proportion to their actual cost or seriousness.

A larger issue, like confirmed galvanized supply lines nearing the end of their realistic service life, isn't something to hide or paper over. Arizona's standard seller disclosure requirements ask about known material defects, and being upfront about a known plumbing age issue, potentially with a recent quote in hand for what a repipe would cost, often produces a smoother negotiation than a buyer discovering it independently and feeling blindsided.

Getting Ahead Of A Buyer's Inspection

If you already know your home has original galvanized plumbing or an aging water heater, getting your own assessment done before listing means you control the narrative and the documentation, rather than reacting defensively to a buyer's inspector's findings during an already time-pressured negotiation period.

A seller who can hand a buyer a recent camera inspection report or a real repipe quote, even if the buyer ultimately asks for a credit based on it, generally comes across as more transparent and trustworthy than one who seems surprised by the same finding.

Should You Actually Repipe Before Selling?

This depends heavily on your specific market and timeline. In some cases, a completed repipe is a genuine selling point worth the upfront cost, particularly in a slower market where buyers have more leverage to ask for concessions anyway.

In a faster-moving market, disclosing the issue honestly and letting a buyer factor it into their offer, rather than spending on a repipe you might not fully recoup, is sometimes the more financially sensible path. There's no universal right answer here, it depends on your specific situation.

Getting A Pre-Listing Assessment

A pre-listing plumbing assessment, covering pipe material identification, water heater age, fixture function, and a sewer camera inspection on an older home, gives you real information to make these decisions from rather than guessing. How This Differs From A Buyer's Perspective

A lot of what buyers actually check for overlaps with what's covered here, pipe material, water heater age, sewer line condition. The difference here is timing and leverage: as a seller, doing this work before a buyer's inspection means you're the one framing the conversation and controlling which fixes happen on your terms rather than reacting to someone else's findings under time pressure.

A Word On Timing Relative To Your Listing Date

Ideally, a pre-listing plumbing assessment happens a few weeks before you plan to list, giving you enough runway to address anything straightforward before photos and showings begin, while still leaving room to make a considered decision about anything bigger, like whether a partial or full repipe genuinely makes sense for your specific situation.

Waiting until an offer is already in hand to discover a real issue puts you in a reactive position during exactly the part of the process where you'd rather have room to think clearly rather than negotiate under a deadline.

What To Do With The Assessment Results

Once you have a clear picture of your home's plumbing condition, the decision about what to actually address before listing comes down to your specific market, your timeline, and your budget, there's genuinely no single right answer that applies to every seller.

What matters most is that the decision gets made deliberately, with real information in hand, rather than by default because no one looked closely enough to know there was a decision to make in the first place.

Working With Your Real Estate Agent

Sharing your plumbing assessment results with your listing agent early lets them help frame the disclosure and marketing strategy appropriately, whether that means highlighting a completed repipe as a selling point or preparing talking points for a known original-era system. An agent who's caught off guard by a buyer's inspection finding is at a real disadvantage compared to one who already knew what to expect.

A short conversation with your agent before listing about exactly what the assessment found, and how you'd like to handle it, avoids surprises for everyone involved once showings actually begin.
