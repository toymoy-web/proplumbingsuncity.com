PEX vs. Copper: A Repiping Guide For Sun City Homeowners

By the Sun City Pro Plumbing team · September 14, 2026

If you're planning a repipe to replace original galvanized supply lines, the PEX-versus-copper decision matters more in a hard water climate like this one than most generic buying guides let on.

What Each Material Actually Is

PEX, short for cross-linked polyethylene, is a flexible plastic tubing that's become the dominant residential repiping material over the past two decades. Copper, by contrast, has been a trusted plumbing standard since the mid-20th century, valued for its durability and long track record.

Both are genuinely solid choices for a modern repipe, replacing the galvanized steel common in Sun City's original construction. The right one for your home comes down to a handful of real, practical differences rather than one being simply better than the other.

What To Do Before You Choose A Material

Your Home's Layout

Tight, hard-to-access spaces favor PEX's flexibility; exposed runs may favor copper's finished look.

Local Fire Code Considerations

Copper's non-combustible nature matters more in specific high-risk applications; ask if this applies to your home.

Your Budget

Get real quotes for both before deciding, since the cost gap varies by home size and layout.

How Long You Plan To Stay

A longer time horizon changes how much weight to put on each material's long-term track record.

Cost Differences

PEX generally costs less than copper, both in material price and installation labor, since it's lighter, easier to route through tight spaces, and requires fewer joints and fittings than rigid copper pipe. For a whole-home repipe, that difference adds up to a meaningful gap in total project cost.

Copper's higher price reflects both the material itself and the more labor-intensive soldering process required at every joint, which takes longer than PEX's push-fit or crimp connections.

How Each Handles Hard Water

This is where the comparison matters most for a West Valley home specifically. Copper can develop pinhole leaks over time from internal corrosion, a process that hard water's mineral content can accelerate, similar in principle to what happens with galvanized steel, just on a much longer timeline.

PEX doesn't corrode the way metal pipe does, since it's a plastic material rather than metal, which removes that specific failure mode from the equation entirely. It's part of why PEX has become the more commonly recommended material for a hard-water-area repipe.

Installation Disruption

PEX's flexibility is a real practical advantage during installation. It can often be snaked through existing wall cavities and around obstacles with far less demolition than rigid copper pipe requires, since copper needs a straighter path and more access points for soldering joints.

For a Sun City home where minimizing drywall and slab disruption matters, that flexibility can meaningfully shorten both the project timeline and the amount of patching and repainting needed afterward.

When Copper Still Makes Sense

Copper remains a reasonable choice for a homeowner who values its longer overall track record or who has specific concerns about PEX in certain applications, like areas with elevated fire risk where copper's non-combustible nature is a genuine advantage.

It's also sometimes the better fit for exposed runs where copper's rigidity and finished appearance matter, versus PEX which is typically run concealed inside walls and slabs.

Making The Decision For Your Home

For most Sun City homes dealing with original galvanized supply lines and this area's genuinely hard water, PEX is the more commonly recommended choice, largely because it sidesteps the metal corrosion story entirely rather than trading one metal's failure mode for a slower version of the same thing.

That said, this is a decision worth making with your specific home's layout and your own priorities in mind, not a one-size-fits-all answer. Our repiping and whole-home pipe replacement page covers real cost ranges for both materials in more detail.

A Real Installation Standard, Not Just A Material Choice

Whichever material you choose, the installation itself is completed to current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for pipe sizing, support spacing, and connection methods. This matters more than it might seem, since an improperly supported or undersized line can cause real problems years down the road regardless of how good the pipe material itself is.

We size every repipe to your home's actual fixture count and water demand, not a generic template, since undersizing a line to save on material cost is a real, avoidable mistake that shows up as poor water pressure at the far end of the house.

What Happens To The Old Pipe

During a repipe, old galvanized or failing copper sections are removed as the new line is run, rather than left capped off and abandoned in place. Leaving old pipe in the walls isn't just wasteful, it can occasionally create confusion during future work if someone assumes an old line is still active.

For sections that are genuinely inaccessible without significant additional demolition, we'll discuss the tradeoff directly with you rather than making that call unilaterally, since sometimes leaving a truly inaccessible short section capped and abandoned is the more sensible choice than opening up a finished wall unnecessarily.

Planning Around The Disruption

Even with PEX's flexibility minimizing the worst of it, a whole-home repipe does mean living with some disruption for a few days, water shut off in stages as sections are completed, and some drywall access points that need patching afterward.

Planning around this, arranging alternate plans for showering if needed, or simply knowing which days water will be off in which parts of the house, makes the process considerably less stressful than going in without a clear sense of what to expect.

A Reasonable Way To Decide

If cost is the primary concern and you don't have specific fire-resistance requirements, PEX is the more commonly recommended path for a hard-water-area repipe like this one. If you value copper's longer historical track record or have a specific reason to prefer metal pipe, that's a legitimate choice too, and we'll install either one to the same standard.
