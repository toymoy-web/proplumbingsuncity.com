How To Read Your Water Meter For A Hidden Leak

By the Sun City Pro Plumbing team · September 7, 2026

Your water meter is the single most reliable tool you already own for confirming whether a hidden leak exists, and checking it takes about ten minutes.

Why The Meter Is The Best First Check

A water bill that's climbed for no obvious reason is a common reason to suspect a leak, but the bill itself only tells you after the fact, once a full billing cycle has already passed. Your water meter tells you right now, in real time, whether water is actively flowing somewhere in your home even when every fixture is off.

This matters most for the kind of leak that doesn't announce itself, a slab leak, a leak inside a wall, or a slow leak in an irrigation line, none of which show up as a puddle on the floor the way a burst pipe does.

Finding Your Meter

Most Sun City area water meters sit in a small in-ground box near the front of the property, usually close to the street or sidewalk, with a metal or plastic lid marked with the utility's name. In our coverage area that's most often EPCOR, though Peoria and El Mirage residents may see their own municipal utility markings instead.

Lifting the lid reveals the meter face itself, along with a small leak indicator, sometimes a spinning triangle or star-shaped dial, sometimes a small digital icon depending on the meter's age and type.

How To Do The Test

1

Shut Everything Off

Turn off every faucet, appliance, and irrigation zone that uses water inside and outside the home.

2

Note The Reading

Write down the exact numbers shown on the meter face, or take a photo for reference.

3

Wait Without Using Water

Let 20 to 30 minutes pass without running any water anywhere on the property.

4

Check Again

Compare the new reading to your first one, and watch the leak indicator dial for any movement at all.

What The Results Actually Mean

What You SeeWhat It Likely Means
No movement at allNo active leak at the time of testing
Leak indicator spinning slowlyA small, slow leak, possibly a toilet or irrigation line
Meter numbers changed with nothing runningAn active leak somewhere in the system
Leak indicator spins fastA larger leak, worth prompt attention

If The Meter Confirms A Leak, What Then

A confirmed leak on the meter doesn't tell you where it is, only that one exists. The next step is narrowing down whether it's inside the house or on the service line between the meter and the house, which is usually done by checking your main shutoff valve.

If you shut off the water at the house's main valve, which is separate from the meter itself, and the meter still shows movement, the leak is on the service line side, between the meter and that valve. If the movement stops, the leak is somewhere inside the home's own plumbing.

A Few Things That Can Cause A False Positive

A Running Toilet

The single most common cause of a small, continuous leak indicator movement, often overlooked as a leak source.

An Irrigation Timer Left On

Check that no sprinkler zone or drip irrigation line is actively running during your test.

A Water Softener Mid-Regeneration

A softener cycling through regeneration uses water briefly and can show as movement.

An Ice Maker Filling

A refrigerator ice maker occasionally cycles water even without anyone using it directly.

What We Do With This Information

If you've done this test yourself and confirmed a leak, that's genuinely useful information to have ready when you call, since it narrows down the diagnostic process before we even arrive. We'll still use electronic detection equipment to pinpoint the exact location, but knowing a leak exists and roughly which side of the meter it's on speeds things up.

Any repair we make, whether it turns out to be a simple fixture fix or a full section of pipe, is completed to current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements.

A Simple Toilet-Specific Test

Since running toilets are such a common cause of a false-positive leak reading, it's worth ruling them out directly before assuming a bigger problem exists. A few drops of food coloring added to the tank, left for about ten minutes without flushing, will show up in the bowl if the flapper is leaking water through even when it looks fully closed.

This test takes almost no time and rules out one of the most common, least expensive causes of unexplained water use before you spend energy worrying about a hidden slab leak or service line issue that may not actually be there.

Why Meter Checks Matter More In An Older Sun City Home

In a newer home, an unexpected meter reading is more likely to trace back to something simple, a running toilet or an irrigation timer left on. In an original Sun City home with galvanized or aging copper supply lines, it's worth taking a positive reading more seriously, since it's genuinely more likely to indicate an actual pipe issue rather than just a fixture problem.

That doesn't mean panic at the first sign of meter movement, but it does mean following up with an actual professional diagnosis sooner rather than waiting to see if the next water bill looks normal again on its own.

Keeping A Simple Record Over Time

If you want to be proactive rather than just reactive, jotting down your meter reading on the same day each month takes under a minute and builds a simple record you can compare against your water bill. A sudden jump between two otherwise-similar months is often the earliest, cheapest warning sign available to a homeowner, well before a bill or a visible leak would tell you the same thing.

This is a small habit, but it's genuinely one of the more useful ones a homeowner can build, especially in an area where hidden leaks are common enough to be worth watching for proactively rather than only after symptoms appear.
