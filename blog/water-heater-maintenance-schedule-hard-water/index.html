Water Heater Maintenance: A Real Schedule For Hard Water

By the Sun City Pro Plumbing team · October 12, 2026

A water heater in a hard water climate like this one lives a genuinely harder life than the same unit somewhere else, and a real maintenance schedule makes a measurable difference in how long it lasts.

Why Maintenance Matters More Here Specifically

The combination of the West Valley's mineral-heavy water and Arizona's sustained summer heat means a water heater here accumulates sediment and scale faster, and corrodes internally faster, than the same unit installed in a milder, softer-water climate. A maintenance routine that's optional elsewhere is genuinely worth treating as standard practice in Sun City.

Annual Tank Flushing

Draining a few gallons from the bottom of a tank water heater once a year clears sediment before it builds up into a thick layer that insulates the heating element from the water above it, forcing the unit to work harder and use more energy to reach the same temperature.

In a hard water area, sediment accumulates measurably faster than the every-other-year schedule sometimes recommended in general buying guides. An annual flush is the more realistic interval for keeping a tank unit performing efficiently here.

Anode Rod Inspection Every 2-3 Years

The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod inside the tank designed to corrode instead of the tank itself, a real, deliberate piece of engineering that protects your water heater's lining. Once the rod is fully consumed, the tank itself becomes the next thing corrosion attacks.

Checking the rod's condition every two to three years, and replacing it once it's substantially consumed, is one of the more cost-effective maintenance steps available, a relatively small part replacement that can meaningfully extend the life of the entire unit.

Testing The Temperature And Pressure Relief Valve

What It Does

Releases pressure automatically if the tank's internal pressure or temperature exceeds a safe threshold.

Why It Matters

A stuck or failed valve is a real safety concern, not just a performance issue.

How Often To Test

Once a year, alongside your other annual maintenance.

What To Watch For

A valve that won't reseat properly after testing should be replaced, not left as-is.

What To Do If Your Relief Valve Fails A Test

Don't Ignore It

A relief valve is one of the few genuine safety mechanisms on a water heater; a failed one needs prompt attention.

Replace, Don't Just Reseat

A valve that leaks or won't hold after testing should be replaced rather than repeatedly adjusted.

Check The Discharge Pipe Too

Confirm the discharge pipe attached to the valve is clear and properly routed.

Call If You're Unsure

This is a quick, inexpensive fix during a routine visit if you're not comfortable handling it yourself.

A Recommended Maintenance Schedule

TaskFrequencyWhy
Tank flush (sediment removal)AnnuallyPrevents insulating sediment layer from reducing efficiency
Anode rod inspectionEvery 2-3 yearsProtects the tank itself from corrosion
T&P relief valve testAnnuallyConfirms a real safety mechanism is still functioning
Tankless descalingEvery 12-18 monthsClears mineral buildup from the narrow heat exchanger channels

Filtration Changes The Whole Equation

None of this maintenance goes away with a water softener or filtration system installed ahead of your water heater, but the intervals stretch out meaningfully. A softened water heater accumulates scale and sediment slower, which means the same annual flush schedule buys more real protection, and the unit overall tends to reach a longer share of its rated lifespan.

If you're weighing whether filtration is worth it purely from a water-heater-longevity standpoint, it's a genuine factor worth including in that calculation, on top of the more visible day-to-day hard water symptoms filtration also addresses.

Signs Your Maintenance Schedule Has Slipped

A popping or rumbling noise during heating is one of the more reliable signs sediment has built up past the point a simple flush alone will fully address. Noticeably longer recovery time between uses, or water that never quite reaches the temperature it used to, both point in the same direction.

None of these signs mean the unit is beyond help, but they do mean it's worth a real assessment rather than assuming the unit is simply reaching the end of its life without a specific, checkable cause.

When Maintenance Isn't Enough Anymore

Even a perfectly maintained water heater eventually reaches the end of its working life, and no amount of flushing or anode rod replacement will fix a tank that's already begun leaking or corroding through. What Happens If You Skip Maintenance Entirely

A water heater that never gets flushed or inspected doesn't fail immediately, it simply reaches the end of its useful life meaningfully sooner than a maintained unit would, often by several years. That gap adds up to real money over a typical unit's expected lifespan, well beyond what the maintenance itself would have cost.

It's also a less predictable failure. A well-maintained unit tends to show gradual warning signs, reduced capacity, minor noise, before it actually fails, while a neglected unit is more likely to fail suddenly, sometimes with an active leak that causes real water damage on top of the replacement cost itself.

Setting Up A Realistic Routine

The honest reality is that most homeowners don't want to think about water heater maintenance until something goes wrong. A simple way to make this manageable is bundling it with something you're already doing annually, a spring cleaning routine, an annual HVAC service call, or simply a fixed date on the calendar each year.

If keeping track of the schedule yourself isn't appealing, we can also handle this as part of a scheduled annual visit, similar to how we work with commercial property owners on a maintenance routine, just scaled to a single residential water heater.

A Realistic Cost-Benefit View

The full annual maintenance routine described here, a flush, a periodic anode rod check, and a relief valve test, typically costs a small fraction of a full water heater replacement. Stretched across even just a couple of extra years of useful life gained, the math generally favors staying on top of it rather than skipping maintenance and hoping for the best.

This isn't a guarantee that maintenance prevents every possible failure, some units simply reach the end of their life regardless of care, but it meaningfully shifts the odds in your favor in a climate that's already working against your water heater harder than most.
