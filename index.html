Built For Sun City's Original Housing Stock

Sun City was built starting in the early 1960s as one of Arizona's first planned retirement communities, and a real share of its homes still run on their original galvanized steel or cast iron supply lines.

Industry service-life guidance puts those materials at roughly 50 to 70 years, which means a large portion of Sun City's original plumbing is now at or past that mark, and it lines up with how often drain and sewer line calls come from the community's oldest sections.

Beneath those slab foundations sits caliche, a dense, mineral-cemented desert hardpan that resists digging and shifts unevenly as summer temperatures swing, which can stress the plumbing penetration points in an older slab over time. The West Valley's water is also notably hard, so scale builds up steadily inside older pipes and water heaters and shortens their working life.

Together, these three factors are why drain clearing, sewer line repair, and whole-house filtration are the services we're called for most in Sun City. When we repipe a home off its original galvanized line, the replacement work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Arizona has adopted, so the new lines meet the same standard a fresh-built home would.

Before We Arrive: What To Do In A Plumbing Emergency

A few quick steps can limit damage while you wait for us, whether that's minutes or a bit longer during a busy storm night.

Shut Off The Water

Know your main shutoff valve location ahead of time, usually near the water heater or at the street.

Cut Power To Affected Outlets

If water is near an outlet or appliance, shut off that breaker before doing anything else.

Move Valuables Away

Get furniture, electronics, and boxes off a wet floor if it's safe to do so.

Photograph The Damage

A few photos before cleanup help if you file a homeowner's insurance claim.

Neighborhoods And Landmarks We Serve In Sun City

Sun City opened on January 1, 1960. By 1961 its boundaries ran from Grand Avenue south to Peoria Avenue, and in 1968 the community expanded north of Grand Avenue with the circular street pattern it is known for. Knowing which phase a home belongs to is the first thing we ask, because it tells us far more about the pipes than the street name does.

Original Section

Oakmont Recreation Center, the community's first, and Fairway Recreation Center anchor the earliest streets between Peoria Avenue and Grand Avenue, built from 1960.

107th Avenue Corridor

Mountain View Recreation Center, opened in 1967, and the Sun Bowl Amphitheater sit along the late 1960s build-out.

North Of Grand Avenue

Lakeview Recreation Center and Banner Boswell Medical Center anchor the expansion that began in 1968.

Bell Road Corridor

Bell Recreation Center, with its library branch and Welcome Center, sits along Bell Road.

Sundial And Marinette

Sundial Recreation Center, home of the Sun City Mineral Museum, and Marinette Recreation Center at the northern edge round out the map.

Just Outside Sun City

Youngtown and El Mirage lie to the south and southwest, Sun City West and Surprise, including Sun City Grand, to the west, and Peoria to the east.
