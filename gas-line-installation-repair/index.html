Gas Line Work

Gas Line Installation & Repair in Sun City, AZ

Whether you smell gas, need a line run for a new range or fireplace, or are converting a water heater to gas, this work requires the same licensing and code compliance as the rest of what we do.

☎ Call (623) 301-9042

What To Do If You Smell Gas

Leave The House

Get everyone out immediately, don't stay inside to investigate.

Don't Flip Any Switches

Avoid light switches, phones, or anything that could spark near the leak.

Call Your Gas Utility First

Their emergency line is the fastest way to get the supply shut off.

Call Us Once It's Safe

We handle the repair once the utility has confirmed it's safe to proceed.

What We Handle

Outside of an active gas smell, most gas line calls are about installing a new line for an appliance, like a range, dryer, or fireplace, or replacing an aging line during a larger renovation.

We install new gas lines for ranges, dryers, water heaters, and fireplaces, and repair or replace existing lines that are leaking, undersized, or corroded, all completed to current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and local permit requirements.

Why This Matters More On Older Sun City Homes

A number of Sun City's original homes were plumbed for gas appliances that have since been replaced with different models requiring different line sizing, and an undersized original line is a real, checkable issue rather than a theoretical one.

Corrosion on an original black iron gas line is also something we check for directly, since a line installed in the 1960s-70s has had considerably longer to develop it than a newer coated or PEX line would.

Common Gas Line Projects We See

Range and cooktop installations are among the most frequent gas line requests we get, often from homeowners switching from electric to gas cooking during a kitchen update.

Fireplace and patio heater lines are another common project in Sun City specifically, since outdoor living is a real part of daily life here for much of the year, and running a dedicated line does it properly rather than relying on a portable tank setup.

Whatever the appliance, the process is the same: assess the existing supply, size the new line correctly, and pressure test everything before it's put into use.

For a whole-house generator or an outdoor kitchen setup, both of which come up occasionally in this market, we size the line to handle the combined load of everything it needs to supply, not just today's single appliance.

Why Sizing Matters

An undersized gas line causes real performance problems, weak burner flame, an appliance that can't reach full output, or intermittent pilot issues under load, none of which show up until the appliance is actually running at demand.

Getting the sizing right the first time avoids a callback down the road once you notice the new appliance isn't performing the way it's supposed to.
