Disposal Repair & Install

Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Sun City, AZ

A garbage disposal that's jammed, humming without spinning, or leaking under the sink is usually a same-visit fix, and a full replacement isn't much more involved when the unit's simply worn out.

☎ Call (623) 301-9042

Signs Your Disposal Needs Attention

A disposal that hums but doesn't spin is almost always jammed, not electrically dead, and it's often a five-minute fix once we're on-site with the right tool.

Water pooling under the sink near the disposal usually means a failed seal inside the unit, which typically means replacement rather than repair.

A disposal that trips the breaker repeatedly is a real warning sign worth addressing before it's ignored into an electrical problem.

What We Fix

We clear jams, replace failed seals and motors, reset tripped units safely, and install new disposals when repair doesn't make sense anymore.

We also reconnect disposals and dishwasher drain lines that have come loose, a common finding alongside a disposal complaint.

Why Disposals Fail Here

Hard water mineral buildup around the disposal's internal components is a real factor in premature failure across the West Valley, on top of the ordinary wear from years of use.

In Sun City's original kitchens, a disposal is sometimes the only appliance under the sink that's been replaced at all, while the supply and drain lines around it are original, so we check those connections too rather than assuming they're unrelated.

What Not To Put Down A Disposal

Fibrous Vegetables

Celery, corn husks, and similar fibers wrap around the blades and cause jams.

Grease And Oil

It solidifies inside the drain line further down, contributing to clogs well past the disposal itself.

Coffee Grounds

They accumulate into a dense sludge that's a common cause of slow drains near the kitchen sink.

Bones Or Fruit Pits

Too hard for most residential disposals and a common cause of motor strain over time.

How Long A Disposal Should Last

A residential garbage disposal typically lasts 8 to 12 years with normal use, though hard water and heavy daily use can shorten that in the West Valley specifically.

If your disposal is already in that age range and showing any of the signs above, replacement is often the more sensible call than another repair, since a second repair on a unit that old rarely buys much more time before something else fails.

A slightly higher horsepower unit is often worth the small extra cost at replacement time, since it handles the kind of fibrous scraps and coffee grounds listed above with noticeably fewer jams than a base-model disposal.

A Quick Reset Before You Call

Check The Reset Button

Most disposals have a small red reset button on the bottom, press it once if the unit tripped.

Never Reach In By Hand

Use an Allen wrench in the bottom hex socket to clear a stuck jam, never your fingers.

Run Cold Water During Use

Cold water helps solidify grease so it grinds and flushes rather than coating the blades.

When To Just Call Instead

If the reset button doesn't help or you're not comfortable using an Allen wrench near the blades, that's exactly the point to call rather than keep troubleshooting on your own.

Every disposal we install is connected to the drain line to current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) trap and venting requirements, which matters most on an original 1960s kitchen where the drain configuration may predate a disposal ever being added.
