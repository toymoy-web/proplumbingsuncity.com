Signs You Need Faucet Or Sink Repair

A faucet that drips even when fully shut off usually means a worn washer or cartridge inside the handle, not a problem with the water supply itself.

A supply line that's damp, corroded, or visibly bulging under the sink is worth addressing before it fails outright, especially on original 1960s-70s valves that were never designed to last this long.

A sink that drains slowly right at the fixture, rather than further down the line, is usually a P-trap clog, not a main line issue, and is a quick fix compared to a deeper drain call.

What We Repair

We repair and replace kitchen and bathroom faucets, fix leaking or corroded supply lines and shutoff valves, and clear P-trap clogs directly under the sink.

If your existing shutoff valve is original to the home and difficult to turn, we'll flag that during the visit. A valve that won't fully close is a real risk during any future repair, plumbing or otherwise.

Why This Comes Up So Often In Sun City

Faucets and shutoff valves in Sun City's original homes are now well past the point where internal washers, cartridges, and valve seats commonly wear out, and the area's hard water accelerates mineral buildup inside all of these components.

A corroded shutoff valve under an original sink is a specific, recurring finding for us here, since these valves were rarely designed with 50-plus years of hard water exposure in mind. We flag a stiff or corroded valve even when it isn't the reason for today's call, since replacing it proactively is far cheaper than an emergency shutoff failure later.

Emergency: A Valve Or Supply Line That Will Not Shut Off

Some of the fastest floods start small. A tub valve that will not shut off, a toilet supply valve that cracks, a stop valve that breaks while something heavy is moved near it or a line that begins spraying inside a wall can put a lot of water on the floor in minutes.

When the local valve will not close, shut off the main water valve and call us. We dispatch 24/7, replace the failed valve or supply line and test it before we leave.

When people ask what it costs to replace a faucet, they usually mean the whole job and not just the visit. A clear estimate lists the faucet itself, the labor, new supply lines and a shutoff valve if the old one is worn.

If you have chosen a fixture, tell us and we price around it. If you have not, we can recommend a durable option that suits your sink and your hard water.

Hose Bibs, Utility Sinks And Washer Valves

Outdoor hose bibs are exposed to sun and heat all year, and their handle packing and vacuum breakers wear out. A drip at the handle or a spray from the body is a quick repair or a replacement.

Utility sinks and the valves behind a washing machine leak slowly and often go unnoticed behind the machine. We check them whenever we are in the area, since a leak there can run for weeks.

Kitchen vs. Bathroom Faucet Differences

A kitchen faucet sees more daily use and more temperature swings than a bathroom faucet, so its internal cartridge tends to wear out first, even in a home where every faucet is the same age.

A bathroom faucet, especially in a guest bathroom used less often, more commonly shows scale buildup from sitting with hard water in the lines between uses rather than pure mechanical wear.

Either way, replacing one worn faucet is a reasonable moment to check the others in the house, since they're usually the same age and material and tend to fail on a similar timeline.

This is worth mentioning when you call, since checking a second faucet while we're already on-site is far more efficient than scheduling a separate visit a few months later.
